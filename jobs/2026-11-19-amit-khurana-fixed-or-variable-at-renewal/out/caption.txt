Fixed or variable at renewal: it takes three quarter-point hikes before variable costs more than fixed (insured rates, Nov 18).
Amit Khurana, Mortgage Agent Level 2 · Lic. M12000871 · Centum Indigo Mortgages Corp. · Brokerage Lic. #12141

Illustrative Mississauga renewal: $420,000 left on a 20-year amortization. The insured 5-year fixed at 4.34% works out to about $2,612 a month. Variable at 3.65% (prime 4.70% minus 1.05%) is about $2,462, so variable starts $150 a month lower.

Each 0.25% hike adds about $54 a month on this mortgage. After two hikes variable is still $41 cheaper. The third takes it to $2,626, past fixed.

The Bank of Canada raised its rate on Oct 28 and decides again on Dec 9. Fixed rates follow bond yields, so they can move before then too.

Over five years of interest: if rates hold, about $69,300 on variable. One hike that sticks, about $74,100. Three hikes in the first six months, about $83,100. Fixed, about $82,800. If rates fall, variable gets cheaper still. Fixed gives you a payment that can't move, which may suit a tight budget.

Uninsured renewals are priced higher and the gap is wider. nesto showed 4.95% fixed and 4.10% variable on Nov 19, a 0.85-point gap, so it takes four hikes.

Your own number: take the fixed and variable rates on your renewal offer, subtract, divide by 0.25 and round up.

Send this to whoever signs the renewal with you.
